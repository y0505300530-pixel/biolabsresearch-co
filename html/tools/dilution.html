How it works

C1 × V1 = C2 × V2, so stock volume V1 = (C2 × V2) ÷ C1.

Diluent volume = V2 − V1. Dilution factor = C1 ÷ C2.

Serial steps (optional): after n folds of factor f, concentration = C1 ÷ fn.

Worked example

Stock 5 mg/mL, desired concentration 0.5 mg/mL, final volume 2 mL → 0.2 mL stock + 1.8 mL diluent (10×).

mg/mL laboratory FAQ

How is mg/mL concentration calculated for research?

mg/mL is milligrams of research compound per millilitre of solution. When a stock already exists, this page uses C1V1 = C2V2: stock volume V1 = (C2 × V2) ÷ C1, and diluent volume = V2 − V1. Use the same concentration units for C1 and C2 (examples on this page use mg/mL). This is laboratory notebook arithmetic only — not a protocol and not medical advice.

What does a 10× mg/mL dilution look like on paper?

Example: stock 5 mg/mL, desired 0.5 mg/mL, final volume 2 mL → 0.2 mL of stock plus 1.8 mL diluent (factor 10×). Serial steps (optional) divide concentration by the fold factor at each step. Figures are research calculations on this device only.

Is this medical advice or a use instruction?

No. The helper is for laboratory research calculations on research compounds. It does not recommend volumes for any organism, and it is not a substitute for your own notebook, SOP, or lot paperwork. Certificates are on request.
