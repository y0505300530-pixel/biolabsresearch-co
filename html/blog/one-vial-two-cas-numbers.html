What a blend COA must show when two compounds share a label.

CAS Registry Numbers index defined substances (or defined salts), not storefront mix names. When two peptides share one vial, the correct label is two CAS numbers — one for each component — plus the sequences and theoretical masses that go with them. Mixing two reagents does not mint a third registry entry.

Each component on a blend carton needs its own identity line before inventory booking.

CAS indexes substances, not mix names

Trade-name blends that never name components have no CAS until a lot COA lists molecules. Purchasing language that treats “Blend X” as if it were a single registry substance creates documentation risk: receiving, LIMS, and SDS mapping all depend on knowing which substances are present.

What a usable blend COA shows

  • Sequence (or structure) for each component
  • CAS (or equivalent registry) for each component when applicable
  • Observed mass for each component
  • Chromatogram information readable against those named peaks
  • Component amounts only when measured for that lot

One sequence line with two peaks, or two masses with only one CAS, is incomplete lot paper. HPLC area% on a blend is still a UV ratio under one method — two main peaks can yield two area% values and still tell you nothing about milligrams of either peptide. Read HPLC area percent vs net peptide content before converting a chromatogram ratio into a weighing factor.

Catalog example: BPC-157 / TB-500 blend — two sequences, two masses, two CAS lines on proper lot paper.
