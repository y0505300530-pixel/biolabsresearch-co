Confirm CAS 2381089-83-2, then store the lyophilized cake

Retatrutide is one of the most requested research reagents we list — and one of the most mislabeled. This is a lab-facing identity and storage note, not a protocol.

What is retatrutide?

Retatrutide (development code LY3437943) is a synthetic analogue discussed in the literature as engaging three receptor pathways — GLP-1, GIP, and glucagon — from one backbone. That is identity, not a ranking against other catalogue names. The CAS Registry Number used on receiving paperwork for this molecule is 2381089-83-2. A seller who only writes “GLP-1” on the carton may be describing a different substance.

Published trial literature exists for LY3437943. That literature identifies the compound under study. It does not change how this storefront lists the material: a laboratory reagent, with lot paper on request. Confirm the CAS, sequence or structure line, and observed mass on the certificate before the vial is booked into inventory.

3 things to check before you request a lot from anyone

  1. They name the real molecule. The listing should say retatrutide (CAS 2381089-83-2), not a family nickname. A cheaper analogue can hide under a trendy label if the CAS is missing.
  2. You can get a lot document with the vial. The document should show testing results for your lot — and the lot number on the paper must match the lot number on the vial.
  3. They answer questions. If a seller gets vague when you ask about testing or storage, expect the same vagueness if documentation is incomplete later.

How to store it so the reagent stays usable

Identity comes first, then storage. Match CAS 2381089-83-2 on the inbound paper to the carton before the cake goes in the freezer. This part is simple, but it is where many labs quietly ruin vials:

  • While it’s a lyophilized powder (as it arrives): keep it frozen (−20 °C), away from light, until your protocol is ready. Don’t leave it on the bench “just for a bit.”
  • After reconstitution: the clock starts. Keep it refrigerated and use it within the window your protocol calls for. Once mixed, a vial is no longer sealed the same way.
  • Visual check: if a reconstituted solution looks cloudy, has floating particles, or changes color — do not use that preparation. No paperwork excuses a vial that looks wrong.
  • Aliquots: if your SOP splits a reconstituted preparation, label each tube with the same lot number as the parent vial. A tube without a lot number is no longer traceable identity.

Do not copy storage folklore from a forum thread onto a receiving SOP. Follow the lot paper and your laboratory procedure. Certificates are available on request for documented lots.

The bottom line

Retatrutide is an actively discussed research molecule, and demand for documented lots is high. Request it where every vial can be documented, and store it like the reagent matters — because it does.
