TB-500 vs Thymosin β4: Which Sequence Is on the Vial

August 31, 2026·BioLabs Research Scientific Editorial·8 min read
BioLabs Research Scientific Editorial Catalog identity notes

Fragment vs full-length identity diagram

Two different molecules share the shop name “TB-500.” Full-length thymosin β4 is a 43-residue actin-binding peptide, commonly indexed as CAS 77591-33-4. BioLabs Research lists TB-500 as a fragment reagent: N-acetyl LKKTETQ (Ac-LKKTETQ), CAS 885340-09-2. Those are not interchangeable identities on a purchase order or a lot COA.

Vendors sometimes print “TB-500” while the certificate still cites the full Tβ4 registry number. For laboratory purchasing, that mismatch is a documentation failure: the mass on the certificate must match the sequence printed on the vial label. If the paper says 77591-33-4 against a heptapeptide fragment carton, request corrected lot paper before the material is booked into inventory.

This note is an identity walkthrough for catalog receiving — how to read the two CAS numbers, what MS should confirm, and how our storefront listing differs from intact thymosin β4. It is not a protocol or a clinical claim.

Laboratory identity check — matching label sequence to lot paper

In practice, purchasing teams compare three fields: (1) the sequence or fragment string on the label, (2) the CAS or registry line on the COA, and (3) the observed intact mass (or fragment mass) reported by the testing lab. Agreement across those three is what makes the lot usable for comparative models. Disagreement means the SKU name alone is not enough.

Public compound records currently index the acetylated heptapeptide Ac-LKKTETQ as CAS 885340-08-9 (PubChem CID 62707662). This catalog prints 885340-09-2 on the TB-500 spec strip. Treat that as a paperwork check, not a reason to ignore sequence: the lot COA still has to show Ac-LKKTETQ and a fragment-scale mass. Intact Tβ4 is a separate record (CAS 77591-33-4; PubChem CID 16132341 / 45382195).

A COA that quotes 77591-33-4 against a fragment vial is the wrong paper. Intact mass vs the sequence on the label is the check. Thymosin alpha-1 is a third, unrelated peptide: 28-residue N-acetyl Tα1.

We do not invent HPLC area% on this page. Purity-related numbers, when issued, live on the lot certificate for that batch. We also do not frame this reagent as an injury or recovery protocol.

Storage follows identity. Keep the lyophilized cake frozen and away from light until the protocol is ready, then reconstitute under your SOP. A fragment vial and a full-length Tβ4 vial are not the same material just because both boxes say “TB-500.” If inbound paper and the carton disagree, do not book the lot; request corrected documents.
