Bacteriostatic water vs sterile water for research

Laboratories often ask whether bacteriostatic water (BAC) or sterile water is the right diluent language for a reconstitution SOP. On this storefront, BAC water appears as a research-solvent catalog add-on; sterile water is content-only (no SKU price). Pair this table with the blog deep-dive.

What differs for research workflows

Bacteriostatic water typically includes 0.9% benzyl alcohol as a preservative system for multi-withdrawal research diluent use. Catalog labeling here uses the research-solvent add-on name — confirm the lot label before preparing stocks.

Sterile water (no preservative) is a different diluent class in lab SOPs. BioLabs does not list a sterile-water SKU; treat sterile water as a content reference and source per your facility’s approved materials list.

Neither diluent changes the peptide CAS on the vial. Solvent choice belongs in the method, not in the peptide identity line. Full narrative: bacteriostatic water vs sterile water (blog).

Which one for your protocol?

BAC / research solvent when the protocol specifies bacteriostatic diluent with benzyl alcohol and you wish to inquire for the catalog add-on lot.

Sterile water when the SOP names preservative-free sterile water — source per your lab materials list (not a BioLabs SKU).

When unsure, align solvent language with the peptide product SDS/COA package and your institutional SOP.
