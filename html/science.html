How a lot is specified

CAS and sequence on the catalog page are public identity. Peak area, observed mass, and net peptide content are lot data. They sit on the COA that matches the vial.

  • HPLC Area percent at a stated wavelength
  • Mass spec Observed mass versus theoretical mass

Overview

What the methods report

  1. HPLC reports area percent. Main-peak area divided by total detected peak area at a stated wavelength. A chromatogram ratio, not the milligrams in the vial.
  2. Mass spectrometry confirms identity. Observed mass is checked against the theoretical mass of the named sequence. Area percent does not name the molecule.
  3. The COA is lot-specific. Lot number, date, laboratory, method, and a chromatogram or peak table travel with that vial.
  4. HPLC percent is not net peptide content. TFA, acetate, and water are often invisible at 214 nm. Net peptide is a separate assay.
  5. Research use only. In-vitro laboratory reagents. Not medicines, not supplements, not for human use.

Questions

Use and limits

What is RUO?

Research Use Only. These materials are sold for in-vitro and laboratory research. They are not approved medicines, not supplements, and not for human consumption, self-administration, or therapeutic use.

Do you dose?

No. No body-weight charts, injection instructions, or treatment advice. The calculator on this page is bench arithmetic (mg/mL) for in-vitro stocks.

Documents

Why two tests?

HPLC reports how UV-active peak area splits. Mass spectrometry checks whether the species matches the expected mass. A complete lot record uses both.

Why is there no purity percent on the homepage?

A percentage without lot, method, and wavelength is not a result. Those figures are issued on the COA for that lot.

What is a lot?

A manufacturing batch. The lot on the vial should match the lot on the certificate. Different lots of the same SKU are different records.

Fulfilment

What does cold-chain mean?

Temperature-sensitive compounds ship with cold-chain packaging rather than as ambient shelf stock. Packaging method, not a stability claim for a named lot.
